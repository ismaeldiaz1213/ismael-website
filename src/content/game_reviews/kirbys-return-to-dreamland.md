## Pre-Game Context 

Back when I was maybe 7 or 8 years old my brother got me a Wii for Christmas. This was my first ever console. If I remember correctly, he ended up being the first one to play something on it -- Mario Part 8 (which is another banger I'll post about later). However, a console doesn't ever truly feel yours until you have a good game to attach yourself to. My sister would solve that problem for me. She had gone to a GameStop one day and came back with a pre-owned copy of Kirby's Return to Dreamland. Immediately it felt so cool to finally be able to say "this is my game".

To anyone who knows her, they'll be shocked to learn that she actually picked up the controller to "show me how to play" and she got past a decent amount of the game. Inveitably she either gave up, got bored, or had something else she needed to do so she handed the controller to me. It was now my time to shine. 

## The Hook
For a first time player, it didn't take long to learn the controls of the game, especially given that a Wii remote doesn't have all that much to interface with anyways. But I think the greatest pull was the mystery of what this pink blob called Kirby was and why he was helping this alien dude. Again, I knew absolutely nothing about the Kirby universe so this intial introduction had a sense of mystery. And despite the fact that this was the Wii era and graphics were in 480p, I was still amazed at the world backgrounds. 


## Gameplay

Now I'll take a moment to pause here and be honest. Is anything here that I am describing something special? No. Frankly there are many games where you could perhaps use the same descriptions I am using. And to some people those games may seem more "fun" given all the action and fast-paced combat that people are used to nowadays. But here's the thing about this game. On the surface it's a very simple game. Press 2 to jump, keep pressing it to bubble your way up. Press 1 to inhale, shake the wii remote to inhale more intensely. Move with the d-pad and boom, you're playing the game. 

And here's the beauty, you could totally get through this entire game by doing the bare minimum. Just scrape by, chill, and relax as you fight mini-boss battles or beat up Whispy Woods for the 3.2 millionth time. But suppose you just pause the game for a sec and then scroll a little while you have a copy ability, you'll soon see something great. There's an entire list of every move you can make and some of them require you to do some pretty interesting button combos or at least have a specific positions for them.


### Highlights

Without spoling all that much (because this game has been remade for the Switch so I think keeping you away from spoilers might still be worth it), I found the plot twist as a kid a little heartreaking. I was genuinely not expecting it. That's a peak highlight for me.

The copy abilities in general are all fun! Some more fun than others but I didn't have any specific copy abilities (other than sleep) that I thought served no purpose.

Also the party games have their own place in the game which are fun for one two rounds and then eventually it's just, "alright what else is there". The Arena serves as a true challenge that I have not had the grit to actually finish. I get a lot reckless damage and you only have so many tomatoes to heal.

### Lowlights

The "hard" mode is more of a, "here's a smaller health bar, go fight these bosses but a little harder". And while that's totally fine, sometimes it I do wish they went all out like they did for that one robot boss battle that had multiple stages. I remember as a kid getting frustrated at the fact that I couldn't beat that guy. Every time I thought I was done, this guy has another stage. And even the final boss had some new tricks that you had to learn, but it wasn't quite new enought to scratch the itch and impulse to do it again.

So if you're at all looking for some serious difficulty... I don't think you'll be losing sleep here.

Speaking of sleep, bruh, why is there a sleep copy ability>:( It's funny though so I get it


## Completion & Replayability

You best believe I completed this game to 100%. And in fact, every couple of years I still replay this game. Now it could mostly be for the nostalgia factor but then again, I don't have that often. There are very very very few games that I will replay and start a save from scratch. And those are usually simulator games but a whole adventure game??? Granted, once I know what to expect from the story and the levels I can almost speedrun this game (perhaps one day I should try to speedrun it lol).

However, if you didn't care for the "collect the gears" and all the other nonsense, I could totally see this being a one and done game. 


## Conclusion

It was my first official video game (according to my memory books), replayable, and just a core memory. My final score shall be: 17/17. Nostalgia aside, perhaps it gets a 13/17.
